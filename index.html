Moshe Mash, Ph.D.

AI Researcher · Multi-Agent Systems, Human-AI Collaboration & NLP

I am an AI researcher working at the intersection of multi-agent systems, game theory, and human-AI collaboration. My work follows three lines of research. The first asks how artificial agents and humans make decisions, cooperate, and reach agreements, both on their own and together, combining game-theoretic analysis with human-subjects experiments and predictive models of human behavior. The second studies how ML practitioners and data scientists build AI systems, analyzing their workflows to predict where they get stuck. The third, an applied line, develops LLM-based methods for understanding specialized text, from authorship attribution in rabbinic literature to textual analysis of patents.

I hold a Ph.D. in Software and Information Systems Engineering from Ben-Gurion University and was a postdoctoral fellow at Carnegie Mellon's Robotics Institute. My work received the Best Paper Award at ACM EC 2016 and was selected as a Communications of the ACM Research Highlight (2018), with publications in JACM, AIJ, ACM TIST, CHI, IJCAI, AAMAS, ACM EC, and Science Translational Medicine, among other venues. I serve as a reviewer for AAAI, IJCAI, AAMAS, KDD, CHI, EC, and ECAI.

Alongside research, I have over a decade of hands-on experience taking AI systems from idea to deployment. At Amdocs I designed and deployed, end to end, a churn-prediction system for at-risk contract renewals that saved millions of dollars and remains in operational use; at Diagnostic Robotics I built clinical risk-prediction models. Since 2022 I have led NLP and LLM research at DICTA, building text-understanding systems for large Hebrew and rabbinic corpora that are in active use by scholars. Through MashInnovateAI, the consultancy I founded in 2024, I help technology companies, startups, and research groups identify where AI can solve their hardest problems, then design and deliver the solution end to end, from applied ML and NLP to LLM-based and multi-agent systems.

I have taught at five institutions, twice received Ben-Gurion University's Excellence in Teaching Award, and mentored students at Ben-Gurion and Carnegie Mellon.

Research Interests

My research centers on decision-making by artificial agents and humans, individually and together, studied through game theory, mechanism design, multi-agent systems, and human-subjects experiments. I am developing a new direction that extends this to LLM-based agents: learning what an agent should communicate to a human, and how, from human preference, in mixed human-agent settings such as autonomous and human-driven vehicles.

A related line of work, begun during my postdoc at Carnegie Mellon, studies the people who build AI systems: capturing and modeling the workflows of data scientists as they develop machine learning models, and predicting the moments where they get stuck so that tools can offer targeted support.

A third, applied strand of my research uses large language models and modern NLP for text analysis in specialized domains: authorship attribution, automatic citation generation, and semantic cross-referencing of Hebrew and rabbinic texts (with DICTA), and computational analysis of legal and patent text, including textual studies of disparate outcomes in the patent system. Both involve fine-tuning LLMs for prediction, clustering, and semantic analysis of domain-specific corpora.

  • Multi-Agent Systems & Game Theory. Cooperation, coalition formation, fair division, and mechanism design among self-interested agents.
  • Human-AI Collaboration. Mixed human-agent settings: what an agent should communicate to a person, and how, including LLM-based agents.
  • Human Behavior Prediction. Models that predict human decisions, learned from experiments with people.
  • ML Practitioner & Data Science Workflows. Capturing how practitioners build models and predicting where they get stuck.
  • LLMs & NLP for Specialized Text. Authorship attribution, citation and cross-reference detection, and textual analysis of legal and patent documents.
